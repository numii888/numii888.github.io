If you're planning a website or app, you've probably heard the terms UI and UX thrown around — often together, as if they're the same thing. They're not. Understanding the difference can help you make smarter decisions when working with a designer, and avoid costly redesigns later.

## What is UI (User Interface)?
UI refers to everything you see and interact with on a screen — buttons, colors, fonts, icons, spacing, and layout. It's the visual language of a product. Good UI makes an interface look clean, modern, and easy on the eyes. Think of it as the "look" of your website or app.

## What is UX (User Experience)?
UX is about how a user *feels* while using your product — how easy it is to find what they need, how smoothly they move from one step to another, and whether the whole experience feels frustrating or effortless. UX is less about visuals and more about structure, flow, and usability. Think of it as the "feel" of your website or app.

## A simple analogy
Imagine a restaurant. UI is the interior design — the lighting, the furniture, the menu design. UX is the entire dining experience — how easy it was to get a table, how quickly your order came, whether the staff was helpful. A restaurant can look beautiful (great UI) but still have a frustrating experience (poor UX) if the service is slow or confusing.

## Why both matter equally
A beautiful website with confusing navigation will frustrate visitors, no matter how good it looks. Likewise, a perfectly functional website with outdated, ugly design won't build trust or make a strong impression. The best digital products combine both: a clean, attractive interface (UI) paired with a smooth, intuitive experience (UX).

## The bottom line
When hiring a designer or evaluating your own website, don't just ask "does it look good?" — also ask "is it easy to use?" Both questions matter, and the strongest brands invest in getting both right.
