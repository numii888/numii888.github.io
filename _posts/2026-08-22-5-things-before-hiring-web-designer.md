Hiring the wrong web designer can cost you months of rework and a site that doesn't actually convert visitors into customers. Before you sign on with anyone, here are five things worth checking first.

## 1. A real portfolio, not just mockups

Anyone can design a pretty mockup in Figma. Ask to see live, published websites they've built — sites that are actually online and working, not just static images.

## 2. They ask about your business, not just your colors

A good designer starts with questions: Who's your customer? What should visitors do on your site? A designer who jumps straight to "what colors do you like" is designing decoration, not a business tool.

## 3. Mobile-first thinking

Most traffic today comes from phones. If a designer can't clearly explain how your site will look and function on mobile, that's a red flag — responsive design should be a given, not an afterthought.

## 4. Basic SEO knowledge

Your site needs to be found. A designer who understands page speed, clean URLs, and basic on-page SEO gives your business a real head start over one who only cares about visuals.

## 5. Clear communication and timelines

Good design work still needs structure. Look for someone who gives realistic timelines, communicates delays early, and doesn't disappear mid-project.

## The bottom line

A website is one of the biggest investments a small business makes in its online presence. Taking the time to vet a designer properly — checking real work, communication style, and technical basics — saves you from costly redos later.
