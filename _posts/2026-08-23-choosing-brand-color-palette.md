Color is often the first thing people notice about a brand — before they read a single word. Choosing the wrong palette can make even a well-designed logo feel off. Here's how to get it right.

## Start with the emotion, not the trend

Different colors trigger different feelings. Blue tends to signal trust and stability (common in finance and tech), green suggests growth and health (wellness, sustainability brands), while red creates urgency and energy (food, retail). Before picking colors, decide what feeling your brand needs to create first.

## Limit your palette

Most strong brands use just 2-3 core colors, plus one or two neutrals for backgrounds and text. Too many colors make a brand feel unfocused and are harder to apply consistently across a website, social media, and print materials.

## Think about contrast and accessibility

A palette might look great in a mockup but fail in real use if text isn't readable against your background colors. Always check that your main text has enough contrast against your background — this affects both usability and how professional the site feels.

## Consider your industry, but don't copy it blindly

It's smart to know what colors competitors use, but standing out can be just as valuable as fitting in. A law firm in warm orange instead of the usual navy blue, for example, can feel more approachable and memorable.

## Test across real contexts

A color palette that looks great on a desktop screen might feel different on a phone, in print, or on a dark-mode browser. Test your chosen colors across multiple devices and formats before finalizing.

## The bottom line

Your color palette isn't just decoration — it's a communication tool. Getting it right builds trust and recognition; getting it wrong can quietly undercut everything else about your brand.
